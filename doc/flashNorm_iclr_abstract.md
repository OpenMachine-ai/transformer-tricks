# FlashNorm: ICLR 2027 abstract draft

Normalization introduces dependencies and intermediate operations into transformer inference. We present **FlashNorm**, a set of algebraic transformations that simplify normalization without retraining. Normalization gains can be folded into adjacent linear weights, and per-token RMS scaling can be deferred until after matrix multiplication, making the reduction and projection independent. These identities hold in real arithmetic, including with epsilon-regularized RMS. We further characterize when an upstream RMSNorm can be cancelled: every affected path must pass through a downstream normalization after a bias-free, positively homogeneous transformation. Cancellation is exact for unregularized RMS; we derive its epsilon-dependent discrepancy for practical implementations. This distinction enables full pre-attention cancellation in eligible QKV-normalized architectures and partial cancellation in multi-head latent attention, where bypassing branches must retain the scalar. On Gemma-4-E2B and Gemma-4-12B, cancellation changes evaluated fp32 token perplexity by less than 0.001% and preserves greedy continuations on the tested prompts. Experiments on MiniCPM3 and DeepSeek-V2-Lite validate partial cancellation and demonstrate severe perplexity degradation from naive full removal. Controlled decode ablations distinguish the effects of fused normalization kernels from weight removal. FlashNorm provides explicit conditions and reference transformations for simplifying transformer normalization while identifying the numerical and implementation constraints that govern practical performance.
